Episode of the day · 🎧

The Very Good and Very Bad News on Climate

Two truths at once: the crisis is accelerating — and clean energy is winning.

The Ezra Klein Show · guest Bill McKibben · 10 July 2026 · ~6 min read

Swipe →

5 / 14 →

The hook

Solar and wind now beat fossil fuel on raw cost — no subsidy required.

For decades clean energy was the expensive, virtuous choice. That has flipped. McKibben’s argument is that the technology has quietly crossed a threshold that changes what is politically and economically possible — even as the physical climate keeps getting worse.

6 / 14 →

The core idea

A new kind of climate politics is possible — one about abundance, not sacrifice.

Instead of framing decarbonisation as disaster-prevention and doing-without, McKibben says we can now present it as a path to somewhere better: a world of energy abundance “where everyone has more, not less.”

7 / 14 →
Finding 1 / 5 · The bad news

Acceleration — and politics in disarray

Climate change is speeding up, and yet, McKibben notes, climate politics right now is in disarray. The paradox the episode sits with: the science has never been clearer and the solutions never cheaper, just as political will has wobbled.

8 / 14 →
Finding 2 / 5 · The good news

Energy abundance is now buildable

Advances in clean-energy technology mean it is now possible to build a world of energy abundance that was, in McKibben’s words, “the stuff of dreams just a few years ago.” The cost curves of solar, wind and batteries have bent far faster than almost anyone forecast.

9 / 14 →
Finding 3 / 5 · The scale

China installs a gigawatt of solar every eight hours

The pace is almost inconceivable: in May, McKibben says, China was building three gigawatts of solar panels a day. That is deployment at a rhythm the world has never seen for any energy source — the raw engine behind the falling costs.

10 / 14 →
Finding 4 / 5 · The geopolitics

China is exporting the transition

Despite the enormous volume of fossil fuels it still imports and burns, China last year exported green technologies worth about half again as much as its fossil-fuel-related exports. Leadership on the energy transition, McKibben argues, is coming on an almost inconceivable scale — from Beijing.

11 / 14 →
Finding 5 / 5 · The reframe

From doom to “more, not less”

The pitch McKibben wants: a climate story that doesn’t only warn of sacrifice and catastrophe, but promises a materially better life — cheaper power, cleaner air, energy that doesn’t run out. Cheap sun, he argues, is the strongest argument the movement has ever had.

12 / 14 →
Why this matters now

The summer of 2026

The conversation was recorded against a backdrop of huge wildfires across the Southwest and Great Plains and an extraordinary heat wave in Europe. The “bad news” wasn’t abstract — it was the weather outside the studio, which is exactly why the “good news” on cost matters more, not less.

13 / 14 →

The takeaway

“Solar is light in dark times.”

— Bill McKibben

Two things are true at once: the danger is rising and the solution is arriving. McKibben’s wager is that the second fact — cheap, abundant clean power, led at scale by China — can finally power a politics of optimism rather than dread.
