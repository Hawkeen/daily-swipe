Episode of the day · 🎧

The Element That Built Our World

Silicon: from dying stars to the microchip — and the ocean’s hidden chemistry

In Our Time · BBC Radio 4 · presented by Misha Glenny · 26 July 2026 · ~50 min listen

Swipe →

5 / 14 →

The hook

One element hides in plain sight — in sand, in glass, in every microchip, and in the shells of the ocean’s tiniest engineers.

In one of the new-look series’ episodes, Misha Glenny and three scientists trace silicon across physics, chemistry and biology — the element “at the heart of some of the most useful and beautiful objects on the planet.”

6 / 14 →

The set-up

Silicon links the cosmos, the microchip, life and the climate — one story told across three sciences.

Glenny is joined by oceanographer Kate Hendry (British Antarctic Survey and Cambridge), chemist Andrea Sella (University College London), and planetary scientist Monica Grady (The Open University) — a line-up spanning the element’s journey from stars to seafloor.

7 / 14 →
Finding 1 / 6 · Origin

Forged in dying stars

Silicon was made billions of years ago inside dying stars, then swept into the material that formed the young Earth. Before it was ever a chip or a pane of glass, it was stardust — a point the episode’s planetary scientist brings down to the ground beneath our feet.

8 / 14 →
Finding 2 / 6 · The chip

Purified into modern electronics

Purified silicon has become the foundation of modern electronics. The same element that makes up ordinary sand, once refined to extreme purity, carries the logic of every computer, phone and data centre — the physical substrate of the digital age.

9 / 14 →
Finding 3 / 6 · Life

A builder of living things

Silicon compounds play a real role in the biology of life on Earth — the strand the episode’s oceanographer draws out. Far from being inert, silicon is woven into living systems, especially in the sea.

10 / 14 →
Finding 4 / 6 · Climate

A quiet lever on the climate

The discussion reaches into climate: silicon’s role bears on carbon-dioxide levels in the atmosphere. The element sits inside processes that help regulate how much CO₂ the planet holds — connecting rock and sea to the air we breathe.

11 / 14 →
Finding 5 / 6 · The cycle

Between land, ocean and air

The programme follows the cycling of silicon between land, oceans and atmosphere — a slow planetary circulation that ties together geology, marine life and the climate system into a single, connected story.

12 / 14 →
Finding 6 / 6 · Why listen

The useful and the beautiful

What makes the episode satisfying is its span: physics, biology and chemistry braided around a single element that sits, as the show puts it, at the heart of some of the most useful and beautiful objects on the planet — from a silicon wafer to a grain of sand.
