Episode of the day · 🎧

After Galwan

Asia’s two giants edge back from confrontation — but the mistrust runs deep

The China-Global South Podcast · with Sushant Singh (Yale) · 10 Aug 2026 · ~5 min

Swipe →

5 / 15 →

The hook

The world’s two most populous nations share a Himalayan border, a history of war — and a deepening wariness of each other.

Can China and India ever be partners rather than rivals? A cautious thaw is under way. Sushant Singh of Yale joins host Eric Olander from New Delhi to map how far it can go.

6 / 15 →

The core idea

Flags of India and the People’s Republic of China. Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons.

China and India are entering a cautious period of diplomatic re-engagement after years of confrontation — but deep strategic mistrust, unresolved border disputes and competing security interests still define the relationship. Economic cooperation is resuming even as the politics stays frozen.

7 / 15 →
Finding 1 / 6 · The shift

Why China’s view of India is changing

Singh argues Beijing’s perception of New Delhi is moving, as shifting global power dynamics reshape the balance between Asia’s two largest powers. India is being reassessed — not as a mere neighbour, but as a swing weight in a multipolar order.

8 / 15 →
Finding 2 / 6 · The freeze

The slow thaw after Galwan

Relations plunged after the deadly 2020 Galwan Valley clash high in the Himalayas. What is under way now is a slow thaw — economic contact resuming while tens of thousands of troops still face off along a contested, unmarked frontier.

9 / 15 →
Finding 3 / 6 · Trade vs trust

Can commerce outrun mistrust?

Economic ties are reviving, but structural rivalry and unresolved boundary disputes make a lasting rapprochement hard. Trade can resume faster than trust — and business ahead of politics is the fragile bargain both sides are testing.

10 / 15 →
Finding 4 / 6 · The triangle

The shadow of the US–China rivalry

The broader US–China contest is reshaping India–China ties — opening room for engagement while sharpening the strategic stakes. New Delhi must court Beijing without alarming Washington, and hedge Washington without submitting to Beijing.

11 / 15 →
Finding 5 / 6 · The chessboard

BRICS, the Quad and Asia’s map

India sits inside BRICS alongside China and inside the Quad alongside the United States — a balancing act across overlapping blocs. Analysts sum up the mood in a phrase: the rapprochement is tactical, not strategic.

12 / 15 →
Finding 6 / 6 · The stakes

Two nuclear giants, one unmarked line

Behind the diplomacy sit the raw facts: two nuclear-armed neighbours, the world’s two most populous states, and a long Himalayan frontier never fully agreed. That is why even a cautious thaw between them ranks among the most consequential shifts in Asia.

13 / 15 →
Why this matters in 2026

Trade reopens on the roof of the world

The episode’s show notes flag the clearest sign yet of the thaw: in 2026 India and China revived cross-border trade at a high-altitude Himalayan pass (South China Morning Post). Goods are moving again where soldiers recently faced off — small, but symbolic.

14 / 15 →

The takeaway

“China–India rapprochement is tactical, not strategic.”

— Chietigj Bajpaee, East Asia Forum (cited in the episode). The thaw is real, but it rests on interest, not trust — and the border, the blocs and the US–China rivalry can refreeze it fast.
