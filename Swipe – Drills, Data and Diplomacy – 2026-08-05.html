Drills, Data and Diplomacy

Three signals from China’s July: warships with Russia, a wobbling economy, a wary Europe

MERICS China Podcast · ‘China in 26: Europe-China relations + Russian-Chinese military cooperation + new economic data’ · 17 July 2026 · ~5 min read

The hook

In one month, China ran joint war games with Russia, posted its weakest growth since 2022, and kept Europe guessing. What ties them together?

The Mercator Institute’s roundup reads China’s July through three lenses at once — hard power, the economy, and the fraught relationship with Brussels.

6 / 13 →

The through-line

A more confident China abroad, a more fragile one at home.

Deeper military coordination with Russia signals geopolitical assertiveness, even as slowing growth and weak domestic demand expose the limits of the economic model. For Europe, that combination — strong abroad, strained at home — is exactly what makes Beijing hard to read.

Finding 1 / 4 · Warships

Joint Sea 2026, off Qingdao

In July the Chinese and Russian navies ran Exercise Joint Sea 2026 in the waters and airspace near Qingdao. Chinese state media reported drills across multiple areas — joint reconnaissance, and air and missile defence — part of the two militaries’ annual cooperation plan.

Finding 2 / 4 · Into the Pacific

From exercise to routine patrol

The drill flowed into a joint maritime patrol that ranged into the Pacific before wrapping up, with submarine participation reported this year. Chinese officials framed the cooperation as safeguarding both countries’ security and regional stability — and insisted it is not aimed at third parties.

Finding 3 / 4 · The economy

Weakest growth since 2022

The new data are sobering: Q2 2026 GDP grew 4.3% year-on-year — the slowest quarterly reading since the end of 2022 and below the ~4.5% forecast. Urban fixed-asset investment fell 5.7% in the first half. Yet exports stayed resilient, jumping 27% in June — strong abroad, weak at home.

Finding 4 / 4 · Europe

Cautious engagement, not reset

After a turbulent 2025 of trade friction and rare-earth disruptions, EU-China relations entered 2026 in a phase of cautious engagement rather than repair. In May, the EU Delegation hosted its second EU-China Conference in Beijing under the telling theme ‘navigating beyond the inflection point’.

Why this matters in 2026

Europe’s two-front puzzle

Brussels has to hold two thoughts at once: a China drilling alongside Russia in the Pacific, and a China whose slowing economy leans ever harder on exports into European markets. Security worries and trade dependence pull in opposite directions — the core dilemma this episode lays bare.

The takeaway

“China-Russia military cooperation is not aimed against third countries.”

— Chinese Defence Ministry, reported by TASS, 17 July 2026

The official line is reassurance; the picture MERICS assembles is more ambiguous. Warships, weak data and wary diplomacy are three readings of the same China — and Europe has to respond to all three at once.
