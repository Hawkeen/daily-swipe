Death at Mayerling

A crown prince, a baroness, and the scandal that shook the House of Habsburg

The Rest Is History · Ep. 690 (Part 2) · 22 July 2026 · ~6 min read

Swipe →

5 / 14 →

The hook

On a January morning in 1889, the heir to a 600-year-old empire was found shot dead beside his teenage mistress.

The Mayerling incident is history’s most consequential lovers’ death: a private tragedy that emptied the Habsburg line of succession — and set a chain of events running toward 1914.

6 / 14 →

The core story

A suicide pact, an imperial cover-up, and a hole at the heart of a dynasty.

Crown Prince Rudolf and Baroness Mary Vetsera died together at his hunting lodge. The court scrambled to disguise what had happened — and in doing so revealed how fragile the seemingly eternal empire had become.

7 / 14 →
Finding 1 / 6 · The heir

A liberal prince at odds with his father

Rudolf was the only son of Emperor Franz Joseph I and Empress Elisabeth (“Sisi”), heir apparent from birth. Unlike his deeply conservative father, he held liberal views closer to his mother’s. By the late 1880s his marriage to Princess Stéphanie of Belgium had failed, his health was collapsing, and he was taking large doses of morphine.

8 / 14 →
Finding 2 / 6 · The affair

A 30-year-old and a 17-year-old

In late 1888 the 30-year-old Crown Prince met the 17-year-old Baroness Marie (“Mary”) Vetsera and began an affair. He had bought the hunting lodge at Mayerling in 1886; it would become the setting for their end.

9 / 14 →
Finding 3 / 6 · The night

30 January 1889

Rudolf spirited Mary to Mayerling on 29 January. The next morning both were found dead of gunshot wounds to the head in an apparent suicide pact: Mary died first, after which Rudolf turned the revolver on himself. He was 30; she was 17.

10 / 14 →
Finding 4 / 6 · The cover-up

“A state of mental unbalance”

Because suicide barred a church burial, Rudolf was officially declared to have been in a state of “mental unbalance” so he could be interred in the Imperial Crypt in Vienna. Mary’s body was smuggled out of Mayerling in the middle of the night, disguised in a fictitious corpse report, and secretly buried at Heiligenkreuz Abbey.

11 / 14 →
Finding 5 / 6 · The proof, 126 years later

The letters in the bank vault

For over a century, conspiracy theories swirled — murder, political assassination, a staged death. Then in 2015 Mary Vetsera’s private farewell letters surfaced in a safe-deposit box in an Austrian bank. They showed she was preparing to die alongside Rudolf, out of love — strong evidence for the suicide-pact account.

12 / 14 →
Finding 6 / 6 · The road to Sarajevo

The empty throne

Rudolf’s death left Franz Joseph with no direct male heir. The succession passed to his nephew, Archduke Franz Ferdinand — whose assassination in 1914 precipitated the First World War. A private catastrophe at a hunting lodge had cleared the path to Sarajevo.
